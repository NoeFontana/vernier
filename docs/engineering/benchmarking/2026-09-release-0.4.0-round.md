80 000 images · 1 240 587 GT boxes · 365 categories. Harness mode `dev`
(N=1); pycocotools needs ~6 min/rep.

| hotcoco | 15.041 s | 15.13 s (+0.6 %) | 8.021 s | 8.17 s (+1.9 %) |
| pycocotools | 367.4 s | 373.24 s (+1.6 %) | — | — |
| faster-coco-eval | DNF (OOM ~30 GiB) | not re-measured ² | DNF | not re-measured ² |

² Reproducing a 30 GiB OOM destabilises the host mid-round; the DNF is
carried forward from the previous round rather than re-measured, and is
labelled as such on the README table too.

**The two competitor arms are the control.** Three measurements across
two thread counts all land within 2 % of the previous round on the same
host, which is what makes vernier's −24.8 % and −32 % attributable to
the code (ADR-0050, ADR-0051) rather than to the machine.

The mechanism is the `load` stage: 0.32 s for vernier against hotcoco's
5.77 s. At `nt=8`, 6.02 s of hotcoco's 8.17 s total is still serial
parsing, so it barely benefits from threads; vernier's ingest is split
across the thread budget (ADR-0054). Peak RSS 2.54 GiB vs 6.49 GiB.

## Ingest surfaces (new in 0.4.0)

ADR-0057's `(N, 7)` matrix route and ADR-0060's `CocoDataset.from_arrays`
carried numbers taken on their own branches. Re-measured on merged
`main` (min of 7, real published GT, every cell verified by
`dataset_hash`):

| cell | `dumps` + parse | `from_arrays` | ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| COCO val2017 bbox-only | 84.4 ms | **4.5 ms** | 18.8× |
| COCO val2017 full segm | 459.5 ms | **43.0 ms** | 10.7× |
| LVIS v1 val bbox-only | 493.1 ms | **32.1 ms** | 15.4× |
| LVIS v1 val full segm | 4 467.9 ms | **355.7 ms** | 12.6× |

The published range is therefore **10.7–18.8×**, and it supersedes
ADR-0060's branch table (12.6–15.7×). The two tables disagree in a
consistent direction — bbox cells faster, segm cells ~21 % slower — and
that is *not* a regression: building the pre-review commit `020a2df` and
running the identical script reproduces the same gap (COCO segm 43.9 ms,
LVIS segm 366.8 ms), so it is a harness difference on the branch, not a
code change. Merged `main` is equal or faster than `020a2df` on all four
cells.

## Two measurement failures worth recording

**A contaminated thread-scaling job produced a fake regression.** The
first thread run reported segm scaling collapsing from 3.08× to 1.84×
and a bimodal bbox `nt=8` distribution. Both were artefacts: the job ran
immediately after ~62 GiB of build-cache deletion and its first two
cells (bbox, segm) were measured against settling IO, while the third
and fourth (boundary, keypoints) were clean. The IQR gate flagged
exactly the affected cells — 10.96 % and 65.68 % on bbox, 11.02 % and
12.60 % on segm — and the clean rerun shows segm scaling *improved* to
3.87× and bbox `nt=8` tight and unimodal at 131.1 ms.

The lesson is procedural: **a failed IQR gate means the measurement is
unusable, not that it needs an explanation.** The bimodality was read as
SMT contention on a 4-physical-core host and written up as "bbox
saturates at `nt=4`". That was a plausible story fitted to invalid data,
and the clean rerun refutes it — bbox scales to `nt=8`.

**The refresh recipe itself was producing `dev`-mode data.** See
[`README.md`](./README.md) §"Refreshing the published numbers" and
commit `f0e39a6`: the recipe omitted `--mode release` under a comment
that said "release", so every cell it produced was a single rep with a
`0 ns` IQR, and it ran `keypoints` against a workload that rejects the
metric. `tools/render_benchmarks.py` now refuses an all-`dev` result
tree outright.

## Cells whose gate failed

Reported rather than re-rolled. `hotcoco` on COCO bbox failed in three
independent runs (5.05 %, 7.69 %, 7.19 %) — that is the impl's own
variance, not the host, since the other three impls in the same
interleaved schedule came in at 1.0–1.5 %. Single-count failures:
`faster-coco-eval` segm `nt=2` (6.18 %) and boundary `nt=2` (8.31 %),
`lvis-api` on the jittered LVIS cell (6.59 %), vernier boundary at one
CPU (5.42 %) and `nt=8` (5.05 %). Treat the gap to the next impl as the
signal in those rows, not the precise ratio.
